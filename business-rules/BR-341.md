# BR-341 — CI privacy-scan job

## Rule

`.github/workflows/ci.yml` has a job `privacy-scan` on `ubuntu-24.04` with `permissions: contents: read`, `timeout-minutes: 20`, no `needs`, no job `if`, no job `env`, and exactly three steps: (1) the BR-350 notice step; (2) the pinned checkout with `fetch-depth: 0` and `persist-credentials: false`; (3) `make privacy-scan`. The job does not run `make setup` (BR-352). Steps 2–3 have the `if` exactly `github.event_name != 'pull_request' || github.event.pull_request.head.repo.full_name == github.repository`. Step 3 `env` is exactly `PRIVACY_DENY_PATTERNS: ${{ secrets.PRIVACY_DENY_PATTERNS }}`, `PRIVACY_EVENT: ${{ github.event_name }}`, `PRIVACY_RANGE_BASE: ${{ github.event_name == 'pull_request' && github.event.pull_request.base.sha || github.event.before }}`, `PRIVACY_RANGE_HEAD: ${{ github.event_name == 'pull_request' && github.event.pull_request.head.sha || github.sha }}`, `PRIVACY_PR_TITLE: ${{ github.event.pull_request.title }}`, `PRIVACY_PR_BODY: ${{ github.event.pull_request.body }}`.

## Rationale

Nothing scanned pushed content or commit messages in this repository, which is to become public; the scan runs on every event and never depends on `changes`, so records-only PRs are scanned too (D-7). Source: [F-023 v1](../features/F-023-hosted-ci-privacy-scan.md).

## Examples

- **Given** the real workflow
  **When** the CI policy validator runs
  **Then** it accepts `privacy-scan` equal to `expectedPrivacyScanJob`
- **Given** `privacy-scan` with `needs: [changes]`
  **When** the validator runs
  **Then** it is rejected
- **Given** the checkout of `privacy-scan` without `fetch-depth: 0`
  **When** the validator runs
  **Then** it is rejected
