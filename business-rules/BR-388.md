# BR-388 — Stdio outbound frames wait in a bounded first-in first-out queue

## Rule

The stdio transport writes outbound frames one at a time in the order `send` is called. A `send` called while a frame is being written joins a first-in first-out queue of at most 6 waiting frames, and its promise resolves once its own frame has been written and drained. A `send` that would make a seventh waiting frame closes the session once with the fixed diagnostic `STDIO_OUTPUT_FAILED`, and that send, the frame being written and every waiting frame reject; nothing is written after close. Each frame is serialized and checked against the 1,048,576-byte bound when `send` is called (an oversized frame closes the session, as today), and each frame's 1,000 ms write bound counts from when that frame's write starts, not from when it joined.

## Rationale

With the invocation queue (BR-380), a response can be produced while the previous one is still being written, and waiters that joined in the same tick time out together; closing the session on a second pending send would turn a parallel call into a lost session. The cap of 6 (`MAX_WAITING + 2`) covers the at most 5 invocation responses of a burst of parallel calls plus frames answered at once without an invocation, and keeps at most 7 frames of 1 MiB in memory (F-026 D-7). Source: [F-026 v1](../features/F-026-bounded-invocation-queue.md).

## Examples

- **Given** a stdout whose write callbacks never fire
  **When** 7 frames are sent
  **Then** the session stays open with 1 frame writing and 6 waiting
- **Given** the same stdout with 7 frames sent
  **When** an eighth frame is sent
  **Then** `STDIO_OUTPUT_FAILED` is written once, all 8 sends reject and stdout is destroyed
- **Given** a first frame drained at 900 ms
  **When** the second frame drains 999 ms after its own write started (1,899 ms)
  **Then** both sends resolve
- **Given** a first frame drained at 900 ms
  **When** the second frame has not drained 1,000 ms after its own write started
  **Then** the session closes with `STDIO_OUTPUT_FAILED`
