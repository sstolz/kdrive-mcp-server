## Rule

Token analysis records the `token_type` Bearer comparison, the presence of refresh and ID tokens, the access-token class (`opaque` or `jwt`) and, for a JWT, `alg`, `typ`, `kid` presence, issuer comparison, audience relation (`resource`, `client-id`, `other`, `absent`), `exp` and `sub` presence and the validator result; it runs a positive check with the owner's allowlists, a negative check with only `qualification-negative-subject`, and the validator on any ID token, reporting both identity checks as `not applicable (unauthenticated)` after a 401; an ID token is usable only with matching `iss`, `aud` containing the client ID and the sent `nonce`; RFC 9207 issuer identification is inconsistent when the redirect `iss` differs, or is absent while the effective flag is `yes` or `unknown`. The `token` step needs at least one allowlist entry.

## Rationale

The verdict and client decisions need evidence of how the real tokens behave, without recording the tokens themselves. Source: [F-008 v1](../features/F-008-oidc-qualification.md), AC-13.

## Examples

- **Given** an opaque access token.
  **When** it is analysed.
  **Then** both identity checks are `not applicable (unauthenticated)`.
- **Given** an authenticated JWT.
  **When** it is analysed.
  **Then** the negative check is 403 `identity`.
- **Given** an absent redirect `iss` with effective flag `no`.
  **When** it is analysed.
  **Then** issuer identification is consistent.
- **Given** an absent redirect `iss` with effective flag `unknown`.
  **When** it is analysed.
  **Then** issuer identification is inconsistent.
