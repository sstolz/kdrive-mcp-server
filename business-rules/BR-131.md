## Rule

On consent approval the proxy discovers the upstream provider through `<upstream issuer>/.well-known/openid-configuration`, usable only when it is a JSON object whose `issuer` equals the configured upstream issuer exactly, whose `authorization_endpoint`, `token_endpoint` and `jwks_uri` are `https` URLs and whose `code_challenge_methods_supported` lists `S256`; the upstream client authentication is `client_secret_basic` when `token_endpoint_auth_methods_supported` is absent or lists it, else `client_secret_post` when listed, else discovery is unusable. A usable result is cached for the life of the process; an unusable or failed one is not cached, is retried at most once per 60 seconds, and produces a client redirect with `error=temporarily_unavailable`. The proxy then stores an upstream flow keyed by a fresh `state` (32 random bytes), valid for 600 seconds, bound to the browser cookie, the pending request, a fresh PKCE verifier (32 random bytes, method `S256`) and a fresh `nonce` (32 random bytes), and redirects (302) to the upstream `authorization_endpoint` with `response_type=code`, the upstream client ID, `redirect_uri=<issuer>/oauth/callback`, `scope=openid` (plus ` email` when the email allowlist is nonempty), `state`, `nonce`, `code_challenge` and `code_challenge_method=S256`, and no `resource`. Every upstream request uses `https` only, follows no redirect, times out after 10 seconds and reads at most 65,536 response bytes.

## Rationale

The upstream leg needs its own PKCE, state and nonce and must not hang or follow redirects to untrusted hosts. Source: [F-010 v1](../features/F-010-oauth-proxy.md), AC-10, AC-11.

## Examples

- **Given** upstream metadata without `S256`
  **When** a consent is approved
  **Then** 302 to the client with `error=temporarily_unavailable`, retried at most once per 60 s.
- **Given** Infomaniak-shaped metadata
  **When** a consent is approved
  **Then** 302 to the upstream with the stated parameters and no `resource`.
