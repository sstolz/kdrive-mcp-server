## Rule

**v1 (F-010 v1; superseded by v2)**

On consent approval the proxy discovers the upstream provider through `<upstream issuer>/.well-known/openid-configuration`, usable only when it is a JSON object whose `issuer` equals the configured upstream issuer exactly, whose `authorization_endpoint`, `token_endpoint` and `jwks_uri` are `https` URLs and whose `code_challenge_methods_supported` lists `S256`; the upstream client authentication is `client_secret_basic` when `token_endpoint_auth_methods_supported` is absent or lists it, else `client_secret_post` when listed, else discovery is unusable. A usable result is cached for the life of the process; an unusable or failed one is not cached, is retried at most once per 60 seconds, and produces a client redirect with `error=temporarily_unavailable`. The proxy then stores an upstream flow keyed by a fresh `state` (32 random bytes), valid for 600 seconds, bound to the browser cookie, the pending request, a fresh PKCE verifier (32 random bytes, method `S256`) and a fresh `nonce` (32 random bytes), and redirects (302) to the upstream `authorization_endpoint` with `response_type=code`, the upstream client ID, `redirect_uri=<issuer>/oauth/callback`, `scope=openid` (plus ` email` when the email allowlist is nonempty), `state`, `nonce`, `code_challenge` and `code_challenge_method=S256`, and no `resource`. Every upstream request uses `https` only, follows no redirect, times out after 10 seconds and reads at most 65,536 response bytes.

**v2 (F-035 v1, #46; accepted on main `703bece`)** — v1 holds, and the production upstream port (`createFetchUpstream`) initiates cleanup on every early exit. When an exchange rejects because `fetch` rejects, the response is a redirect (type `opaqueredirect` or status 300–399), the body exceeds 65,536 bytes, reading the body fails, or the 10-second deadline passes, then before the returned promise rejects it aborts the request's signal and initiates cancellation of any still-readable response body not read to its end, through its owning reader when locked and through the body otherwise. A closed or errored stream needs no cancellation callback; a missing body needs only the abort. Aborting is independent of cancellation settlement: the port never awaits cancellation, so a rejected or never-settling cancellation cannot delay rejection or extend the 10-second deadline. Synchronous cleanup throws and asynchronous cancellation rejections are handled and never replace the original failure. Stream termination, cancellation initiation and eventual transport closure are distinct; no source callback is required after abort has already closed or errored the stream. A URL other than `https` still rejects before any `fetch`. The rejection is otherwise as under v1 (discovery unusable, `server_error` at the code exchange, BR-132). A fulfilled exchange has read its body to its end and does not abort its signal.

## Rationale

The upstream leg needs its own PKCE, state and nonce and must not hang or follow redirects to untrusted hosts. Source: [F-010 v1](../features/F-010-oauth-proxy.md), AC-10, AC-11.

## Examples

- **Given** upstream metadata without `S256`
  **When** a consent is approved
  **Then** 302 to the client with `error=temporarily_unavailable`, retried at most once per 60 s.
- **Given** Infomaniak-shaped metadata
  **When** a consent is approved
  **Then** 302 to the upstream with the stated parameters and no `resource`.
- **Given** the production upstream port (v2)
  **When** the upstream answers 302 with a body
  **Then** the exchange rejects, cancellation is initiated for a still-readable body and the request is aborted
- **Given** the production upstream port (v2)
  **When** the body has 65,536 bytes, then 65,537 bytes
  **Then** it fulfils without abort, then rejects with cancellation initiated if still readable and the request aborted
- **Given** the production upstream port (v2)
  **When** the body's stream errors after 10 bytes
  **Then** the exchange rejects and the request is aborted

- **Given** an oversized source that has already closed or an abort-aware source that errors on abort (v2)
  **When** the exchange rejects
  **Then** the signal is aborted, with no source cancellation callback required
- **Given** a locked readable body whose cancellation throws, rejects or never settles (v2)
  **When** oversize detection or the 10-second deadline rejects the exchange
  **Then** cancellation is initiated through the reader, abort is independent, the original failure is preserved and rejection never waits for cancellation
- **Given** a duck-typed opaque redirect with a readable body, or a bodyless opaque redirect (v2)
  **When** the exchange refuses it
  **Then** the first initiates cancellation and aborts; the second only aborts
